Dental Implants: A Permanent Answer to Missing Teeth

8 May 2026 6 min read

A missing tooth is rarely just a cosmetic matter. The teeth on either side begin to drift, the opposing tooth over-erupts, and the jawbone underneath slowly shrinks. A dental implant replaces the tooth from the root up — which is what makes it the closest thing to growing a new one.

What an implant actually is

An implant has three parts. A small titanium post takes the place of the missing root and is set into the jawbone, where the bone gradually fuses to it over a few weeks — a natural process that gives the implant its remarkable stability. A connector sits on top, and a custom crown completes it. The result looks and works like a real tooth, and it stands on its own without leaning on the teeth beside it.

Implant, bridge, or denture?

All three replace missing teeth, but they do it differently. The right choice depends on how many teeth are missing, the health of the neighbouring teeth, and the bone available.

  • Implant — stands alone, protects the jawbone, and leaves the neighbouring teeth untouched. The longest-lasting option for a single gap.
  • Bridge — fills a gap by anchoring to the two adjacent teeth. Faster and a good fit where those teeth would benefit from crowns anyway.
  • Denture — removable and well suited to replacing several teeth at once, or as a stepping stone while a longer-term plan is arranged.

Why protecting bone matters: because an implant transmits chewing force into the jaw the way a real root does, it keeps the surrounding bone stimulated and healthy — something a bridge or denture cannot do.

Choosing the crown: zirconia, E-Max and PFM

The visible crown — whether on an implant, a bridge, or a damaged natural tooth — can be made from different materials, each with a sweet spot:

  • Zirconia — exceptionally strong and natural-looking, ideal for back teeth that take heavy load and for patients who want a metal-free result.
  • E-Max — a glass-ceramic prized for its lifelike translucency, often the choice for front teeth where appearance is everything.
  • PFM (porcelain-fused-to-metal) — a long-proven, hard-wearing option that balances strength and cost.

Matching the material to the tooth's job and position is what makes a restoration disappear into your smile rather than stand out from it.

Rebuilding a whole mouth

When several teeth are worn, broken, or missing, treating each problem in isolation rarely gives the best result. Full-mouth rehabilitation steps back and plans the bite as a whole — combining implants, crowns and bridges so that the teeth meet evenly, function comfortably, and look consistent. It restores not just individual teeth but the way the whole mouth works together.

The takeaway

Replacing a missing tooth is as much about protecting what remains as it is about filling a gap. An implant does both — and paired with the right crown material, it blends in so well that it becomes the tooth you stop thinking about. The first step is simply a conversation about which option fits your mouth, your bone, and your goals.
